How it works

    
  

  

    

      
      

        
sorted …

        
        
One request for one file on the sorted site, once a day, with nothing about you or the shoot in it. Off unless you tick it.

        

      

    

    

      
      

        
Connect the shoot disk. "Scan a disk or folder" picks the folder and reads every photo and clip once; a big shoot takes a while, and the Scan tab shows how far it is. The disk is only ever read. The Mac stays awake until the scan ends.

        
If the disk is unplugged or the Mac dies mid-scan, nothing is lost: the shoot opens with "2,080 of 4,315 scanned" and a Continue scan button that reads only what is missing. The Scan tab's last line says what is on the disk, what is scanned and what is searchable, with one button for the gap.

        
What the scan learns lives on this Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/photosort. "Save project" (command S) packs it into one file, sorted_<shoot>.sorted, in a sorted folder beside the shoot so it travels with the disk; "Save to" puts it anywhere else, and that place is remembered.

        
Hand that file to an editor with the disk and "Open a project file" (or a double-click on it) opens the shoot on their Mac in seconds, already searchable, with the saved people. Older files named .photosort-index.zip open the same way.

        
Nothing leaves the Mac. The one exception is the version check above, off unless you tick it, and it sends nothing either.

      

    

    

      
      

        
Type what you remember seeing ("woman in a sari", "fishing boats") and press Enter. Photos and clips come back together.

        
Arrow down in the search field lists the last 20 searches on this shoot, with the filters they ran with. The star keeps one as a chip in the sidebar.

        
"Show in Finder" (in the inspector, on the selection bar, or command shift R) opens the file's own folder with it highlighted, the way an editor reveals a clip. Up to 20 at a time.

        
A file that is on the disk twice is one tile marked x2; a burst is one tile marked "burst of 12". The inspector shows the copies, or picks the sharpest frame. Export follows what is shown.

        
The Sharp slider hides the soft ones.

        
Sort puts the grid in order: oldest or newest first, biggest or smallest file, by name, longest clip first. The tile shows whatever it is being sorted on. A search always comes back best match first, so the control greys out until you clear it.

        
Days in the sidebar breaks the shoot into the days it was shot on. Click one to see just that day. A file with no capture time counts on the day it was written, so nothing falls out of the list.

        
Click selects. Shift-click takes a range, ⌘-click toggles one, ⌘A takes everything on screen.

        
"Select all matching" takes every result, not just the 200 on screen.

        
Enter or a double-click opens a shot. ← → step through, Esc closes.

      

    

    

      
      

        
The fixed set is always there.

        
"Discovered in this shoot" is what the model found on its own. Its names are a best guess.

        
"Unnamed group N" means it saw a group but would not guess. Rename it with the pencil; those names are lost when you Categorise again.

        
Everything under 50 % sits in the "less sure" band. It is not hidden.

      

    

    

      
      

        
Group faces once.

        
"Same person?" shows lookalike groups. Same merges them, Different is remembered forever.

        
Name a group by typing in its row.

        
"Find a person from a photo" pulls every frame of them from one reference. Name that result to save the person.

        
Saved people export as their own folders.

      

    

    

      
      

        
The Places tab draws every photo and clip that knows where it was taken, as heat on a world map. Drag to move, scroll to zoom, Fit frames the shoot and World pulls all the way back.

        
Click a pile, or a row in the list under the map, to see exactly those in Search. Shift-drag a box on the map to take an area instead.

        
Phones and drones write the location into every file. Most cameras never do unless they were paired with a phone that day, so a shoot photographed on a body alone will have little on the map and that is the camera, not sorted.

        
Place names come from a list of cities that ships with the app. Nothing is ever looked up: sorted makes no network request, here or anywhere else.

        
A shoot indexed before this existed has no locations stored. "Read locations" goes through those files once and reads the metadata out of them. It only reads.

      

    

    

      
      

        
"Check focus" scores every photo and clip once. Photos are free, clips take seconds.

        
"Hide out of focus" drops the fully bad ones.

        
"Hide soft" also drops the bottom 15 %.

        
Nothing is deleted.

      

    

    

      
      

        
Exports COPY or LINK to the destination. Default is ~/Desktop/photosort-out; change it to any disk.

        
The preset next to the selection's export row sets it up in one go: "Client web set" copies 2048 px JPEGs, "Editor selects" writes links, "Full size copy" copies the originals with their RAW files. Change any control and it becomes Custom. The shoot remembers the last one.

        
The Scan tab lists the last 10 exports: what, where, when and how many. "Show in Finder" opens the folder, "Run again" repeats the export as it was, into the destination as it is now.

        
One folder per ticked category, per person, or for the current selection.

        
"Videos: only the matching segments" trims clips at keyframes.

        
Google Drive as the destination: paste a folder link and the files go straight from the shoot disk into it; nothing is saved on this Mac on the way. What you upload counts against your own Drive space, unless the folder is on a Shared Drive. Uploads are resumable, and a manifest means a re-run only sends what is missing.

        
The project file is what an editor opens to get the shoot already sorted.

      

    

    

      
      

        
The only thing that writes to the shoot disk.

        
Moves files into sorted/ by category (and people, drone).

        
RAW pairs and sidecars stay together.

        
Undo puts every file back exactly.

        
Read the guards before you use it.

      

    

    

      
      

        
While you test, sorted keeps a log on this Mac: which tabs and buttons you used, what you searched for, what was scanned and exported, how long each step took, and every error. No photos, no thumbnails, no names, no file paths beyond the shoot folder's name. Nothing is sent anywhere; the report is a zip you email us yourself.
