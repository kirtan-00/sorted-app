Between the disk and the delivery, five things happen.

  1. Plug in

    The disk mounts read-only. Nothing is written to it, ever.

  2. Index

    Every photo and clip gets a fingerprint on this Mac. 3,677 photos took about 11 minutes on a base M1 with 8 GB.

  3. Search

    Plain words. The matches light up, the less sure ones stay visible under a line.

  4. People

    Every detected face lands in a group. One photo of a person finds the rest of them.

  5. Export

    One folder per category, links or copies, free space checked first. Or one index bundle that opens the shoot already sorted on another Mac.

Every number here was measured on one machine: a 2020 MacBook Air, Apple M1, 8 GB, the base model.

How the processing works

  1. RAW is read from the full-size preview the camera already embeds. No demosaic, so an ARW costs the same as a JPEG.
  2. One small on-device vision model, MobileCLIP, embeds every photo and six sampled frames per clip.
  3. Scene cuts come from a keyframe pass at reduced size.
  4. Faces are found on the preview and matched by a compact face embedding.
  5. Everything runs on the M1's GPU and CPU inside 8 GB. Nothing leaves the Mac, no internet needed.
  6. The index lives in Application Support. The shoot disk is never written to, unless you press Reorganise, which has Undo.

Photos and videos, together.

JPEG, Sony ARW and DNG sit next to h264 and HEVC 4K clips in one grid. A clip gets a whole-clip label and a scene breakdown: every setup in a B-roll gets its own labelled stretch, and you export the whole clip or just the matching trimmed segment. Interviews longer than ten minutes with people talking are flagged interview on their own.

Face detection runs on stills. Clips do not get faces yet; they get the breakdown instead. That is the honest state of the beta.

Every face, one person.

Group faces sorts every detected face in the stills into people. When two groups look alike, the app asks once, Same or Different, and remembers the answer on every regrouping. Drop in one photo of someone and the grid fills with every frame of them. Name them and they are saved with the shoot.

Offline. No data leak. Ever.

Works fully offline. No account needed to sort. No upload, no telemetry. The vision model and the face detector run on the Mac, the index lives next to the shoot, and nothing leaves the Mac, ever. The only bytes that go anywhere are the ones you send to a client's Drive yourself.

The shoot disk is opened read-only and stays byte for byte as it was. Search, people, categories and export never write to it.

The one exception is Reorganise. It renames files into a sorted folder on the same disk, writes an undo manifest first, never deletes or overwrites anything, and Undo puts every file back where it was.
