# Naming the thing currently called photosort

Date: 2026-09-19
Scope: a Mac app for small film and photo studios. Plug in the shoot disk, it indexes everything on-device, you search in plain words, find every shot of a person from one reference photo, get categories it discovers itself, a drone filter, scene breakdown for video, and a one-click export of exactly what the editor needs. Nothing leaves the Mac.

Brief constraints: two syllables (three max), works lower-case as a wordmark, no "AI" in the name, no -ly / -ify suffixes, not a dictionary word already owned by a big brand.

Conflict-check method: WebSearch (US index) for the exact name against photo, video, culling, MAM and Mac apps, then one extra query with "India" appended. This surfaces obvious product and app collisions only. It is not a trademark search; before committing, run the name through ipindia.gov.in (TM class 9 and 42) and the US/EU registries. Nothing below claims any domain is available.

## Ranked

### 1. lightproof

Tagline: Plug in the disk. Search the whole shoot. Nothing leaves the room.

Why it wins: it names the one thing every cloud culling tool (Aftershoot, Imagen, FilterPixel, Narrative) cannot say about itself, and that is the product's actual difference, not its category. It also came back clean on every conflict query, while rushes sits one word from Adobe Premiere Rush and next to a tiny indie app with the same "find it by what it looks like" pitch, and almirah needs a footnote the moment you show it outside India.

Angle: offline and private, told through a craft word. Film bags, changing bags and darkrooms are lightproof: sealed so nothing gets in or out. This app is lightproof in the other direction. No cloud, no account, no upload. A filmmaker gets the double meaning in one beat, and it does not need "private" or "local" or "secure" anywhere in the copy.

Pronunciation risk in Indian English: none. "Lite-proof", stress on the first syllable, same in Ahmedabad and in London. Two syllables. Nothing rude or odd in Hindi or Gujarati.

Conflict check (2026-09-19): no photo, video, culling or Mac app called Lightproof surfaced. Queries returned LightX, Lightricks, Relight, Darkroom and light-meter apps, none of which share the name. The India query returned lighting-fixture brands (Havells, Syska, Wipro) with no "Lightproof" among them. Cleanest of the three by a wide margin.

Wordmark: all lower-case in a humanist monospace (JetBrains Mono or Berkeley Mono), because a mono l is unmistakably an l, and the mono texture reads as "instrument" rather than "app"; optionally the second half in a slightly lower ink weight, "light" solid and "proof" at 70%.

### 2. rushes

Tagline: The rushes came in. Ask them a question.

Angle: film craft plus "sort after the shoot" in one word. Rushes is what Indian crews (and British ones) call the raw footage that lands after a shoot day, the exact pile this app is built to deal with. It names the input, so every feature reads as something you do to your rushes: search them, find a face in them, break them into scenes, export the keepers. Two syllables, sounds fast, reads as a plain noun in lower case.

Pronunciation risk in Indian English: none. "Rush-iz" everywhere. The only wrinkle is that a stills-only photographer may not use the term day to day; a film studio says it constantly.

Conflict check (2026-09-19): crowded neighbourhood, no exact big-brand owner.
- Adobe Premiere Rush (Adobe, video editor): the big one. Different word (Rush, not Rushes) and a different job, but any trademark examiner will look at it, and "is this the Adobe thing" is a question you will answer at every demo.
- First Rush (Mac App Store, on-set multicam SDI recorder, Korea): adjacent pro-film tool.
- Rushes (itch.io, xixgames): a small indie "living wall for your photo album" for Windows, macOS and Android that pitches "find things by what they actually look like". Tiny, but it is the same idea and the same platform.
- RushesLab.ai: AI video generation, surfaces on India-flavoured queries.
- SureShot Video (Google Play, package name com.rushes.production): an Indian production app using rushes in its identifier.
Verdict: usable, but you would be defending it from day one.

Wordmark: lower-case in a compact grotesk with a tight track, like the credit-block type at the end of a documentary; no icon needed, the word already looks like a slug on a mag label.

### 3. almirah

Tagline: Your shoot, in your almirah. Not in somebody's cloud.

Angle: offline and private, in Indian English. An almirah is the steel cupboard every Indian household and studio actually keeps its valuables in: locked, in the room, yours. It says "nothing leaves the Mac" in a word your target studio already uses and that makes a Mumbai post house smile. It also carries a quiet point about where this software comes from, which is worth something when you are showing it to other Indian studios.

Pronunciation risk in Indian English: none, "al-mee-rah", three syllables, spoken daily. The risk is the other way: a non-Indian buyer will say "al-MY-rah" and need one line of explanation, and "almirah" has more silent charm than product authority. It is the most local name and the least exportable one.

Conflict check (2026-09-19): no photo, video or creative-tools product surfaced. Collisions are all in fashion and retail: Almirah (Pakistani eastern-wear brand with its own shopping app), Almirah (Delhi kids' apparel brand, founded 2010), "Almirah Wale" (Indian dealer ordering app), plus several Android apps and IndiaMART steel-cupboard listings. None of those are in class 9 software for media, but the Pakistani fashion brand is well known enough that a Google search for the bare word will not show you first.

Wordmark: lower-case in a warm serif with a real italic (Tiempos, or Martel if you want matching Devanagari; Rosetta's Yrsa has a Gujarati sibling, Rasa, if you ever set it in Gujarati), set slightly wide, because the word is soft and domestic and a serif lets it be that without looking like a Bollywood title card.

## Rejected candidates

- loupe: perfect metaphor (the magnifier you scan a contact sheet with), but two Mac apps already use it for exactly this job: loupephotos.com ("everything stays on your Mac") and tagwithloupe.com (private local tagging). Dead on arrival.
- dailies: the American twin of rushes, but o/DAILIES (MWM), Colorfront Express Dailies, MTI Cortex and an iOS "Dailies" photo diary already own it in both the pro and consumer lanes.
- sortie: sort plus an outing, cute; an iOS "Sortie Photo Cleaner" with an on-device, nothing-leaves-your-phone pitch got there first.
- stringout: the editor's term for a sequence of every usable shot; stringout.com is a Mac MAM with on-device search. Closest competitor, not a name.
- selects: the shots you keep; collides with Narrative Select and Selekt (selekt.photos), both culling tools.
- strongroom: the Indian bank-vault word, good privacy angle; taken by a photo backup client (strongroom.me), an Android photo vault and StrongRoom AI.
- spool: film-reel feel; Spool is a Pixite music-video editor on the App Store.
- wrangle: the DIT's own verb; WrangleBot is a media asset manager, and the jeans brand hovers over it.
- rollcall: face-finding pun on film roll; buried under attendance apps and an Android app that does exactly the face-grouping trick.
- darkroom: the obvious private-room word, but Darkroom is a well-known photo and video editor on iOS and Mac, a dictionary word already owned.
